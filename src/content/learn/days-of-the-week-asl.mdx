---
title: Days of the Week and Time in ASL
---

Time in ASL lives on a line that runs through your body. Behind your shoulder is the past. Right in front of you is now. Out ahead is the future. Almost every time sign moves along that line, and once you feel it, you can read signs you have never seen before just by where they go.

The days of the week are the easy part, so let's start there.

## Monday through Saturday

<Signs ids="monday,tuesday,wednesday,thursday,friday,saturday" columns={3} />

Six of the seven days are the first letter of the English word, circled in front of you with the palm facing the listener. <S id="monday" /> circles an M. <S id="tuesday" /> circles a T. <S id="wednesday" /> circles a W. <S id="thursday" /> is the exception within the pattern: a T that flicks to an H. <S id="friday" /> circles an F. <S id="saturday" /> circles an S.

## Sunday and weekend

<Signs ids="sunday,weekend" columns={2} />

<S id="sunday" /> breaks the pattern. Two open hands, palms out, circle outward, a sign that some say comes from a congregation's hands raised in worship. <S id="weekend" /> is WEEK followed by END, a hand sliding across the palm and then chopping down off the edge.

## Today, tomorrow, yesterday

<Signs ids="today,tomorrow,yesterday,now" columns={4} />

Here is the timeline. <S id="now" /> drops two bent hands in front of you: right here. <S id="today" /> is NOW and DAY together, or just NOW signed twice. <S id="tomorrow" /> moves a thumb forward from the cheek, out into the future. <S id="yesterday" /> moves a thumb back from the cheek toward your shoulder, into the past.

The direction is the meaning. Forward is future, backward is past. That is why you can understand <S id="nextweek" /> and <S id="lastweek" /> the first time you see them: they are WEEK moving forward or backward.

<Note title="Teacher's note">
Time goes first. ASL puts the time at the beginning of the sentence so the listener knows which part of the timeline you are on. <em>Tomorrow I go to work</em> is TOMORROW I GO WORK. <em>I went yesterday</em> is YESTERDAY I GO. Set the time, then say what happened.
</Note>

## Morning, afternoon, night

<Signs ids="morning,afternoon,night" columns={3} />

These show the sun. <S id="morning" /> brings a flat hand up from behind the other arm, like a sunrise. <S id="afternoon" /> holds the hand at a slight angle above the arm, the sun at the top of the sky. <S id="night" /> bends the hand down over the arm as the sun sets. GOOD plus any of these gives you good morning, good afternoon, good night.

## Week, month, year

<Signs ids="week,month,year" columns={3} />

<S id="week" /> slides an index finger across the palm. <S id="month" /> runs an index finger down the other index finger. <S id="year" /> circles one fist around the other and lands on top, one planet orbiting another. Add a number handshape in place of the index finger to say two weeks or three months in one sign.

## Asking what day it is

<Gloss>TODAY WHAT-DAY?</Gloss> with the eyebrows down. Or <Gloss>TODAY DAY WHAT?</Gloss> The answer is just the day sign. To say when something happens: <Gloss>MEETING WHEN?</Gloss> Answer: <Gloss>TUESDAY MORNING</Gloss>.

## Practice

Every morning, sign the day and the time of day: TODAY TUESDAY, MORNING. Then one plan: TODAY I WORK. TOMORROW I REST. Before bed: YESTERDAY I ... and fill it in. That is thirty seconds a day and the whole timeline becomes automatic.
