There's no secret trick to learning ASL quickly, but there are habits that make a real difference. Most of them are about consistency and the way you practice, not how many hours you put in.

## 8 habits that speed you up

1. **Practice every day, briefly.** Ten minutes daily beats an hour once a week.
2. **Review on a schedule.** Bring signs back right before you'd forget them; that's what spaced review does.
3. **Learn in phrases, not single words.** <S id="mynameis" /> ___, <S id="nicetomeetyou" /> sticks better than three separate signs.
4. **Start with signs you'll use today:** greetings, <S id="please" />, <S id="thankyou" />, <S id="yes" />, <S id="no" />, and [question words](/learn/asl-question-words).
5. **Use your face from the start.** Facial expression is grammar in ASL, not decoration. [Facial expressions in ASL](/blog/facial-expressions-in-asl)
6. **Learn from video only.** Movement is half of every sign. Our [dictionary](/dictionary) has a real signer for every clip.
7. **Sign out loud to yourself.** Narrate your day in the signs you know. [Practicing without a partner](/blog/practice-asl-without-a-partner)
8. **Get feedback every week:** a class, a tutor, a signing friend, or a Deaf community event.

## What's realistic

- **Week 1:** the alphabet, your name, and a basic introduction
- **Month 1:** about 100 signs and simple questions
- **A few months:** slow, friendly conversations
- **Years:** fluency, as with any language

More detail in [how long does it take to learn ASL?](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-learn-asl)
