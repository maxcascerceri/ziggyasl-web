---
title: Food and Mealtime Signs in ASL
---

Food vocabulary is the most practical set in the language after greetings, because you use it every day, usually three times. The good news is that most food signs are iconic: they look like eating or holding the thing. The better news is that breakfast, lunch, and dinner are all built from one sign.

## The EAT family

<Signs ids="eat,breakfast,lunch,dinner" columns={4} />

<S id="eat" /> brings a flattened O handshape to the mouth, as if putting food in. <S id="breakfast" /> is EAT followed by MORNING. <S id="lunch" /> is EAT followed by NOON. <S id="dinner" /> is EAT followed by NIGHT. Learn EAT and the three times of day from the [days and time guide](/learn/days-of-the-week-asl) and you have all four.

## Hungry, thirsty, full

<Signs ids="hungry,thirsty,full,delicious" columns={4} />

<S id="hungry" /> draws a C handshape down the chest, like an empty stomach. <S id="thirsty" /> draws an index finger down the throat. <S id="full" /> brings a flat hand up under the chin and stops: nothing more fits. <S id="delicious" /> touches the middle finger to the lips and flicks it away with a satisfied face.

<Signs ids="imhungry,iwanteat,iwantdrink" columns={3} />

The phrases <S id="imhungry" />, <S id="iwanteat" />, and <S id="iwantdrink" /> are how you actually say it: a point to yourself, then the feeling or want. In casual signing the point is often dropped and HUNGRY with a nod is a full sentence.

## Drinks

<Signs ids="drink,water,coffee,tea,juice" columns={3} />

<S id="drink" /> tips a C handshape toward the mouth like a cup. <S id="water" /> taps a W on the chin. <S id="coffee" /> grinds one fist on top of the other, like an old coffee mill. <S id="tea" /> dips a pinched hand into an O, a teabag in a cup. <S id="juice" /> is the letter J at the chin.

<Note title="Teacher's note">
Water, red, and a few other signs all live at the chin, and beginners sometimes blend them. The handshape keeps them apart: W for water, index finger for red. Make the letter crisp before you tap.
</Note>

## Fruit and snacks

<Signs ids="apple,banana,bread,pizza" columns={4} />

<S id="apple" /> twists a bent index finger knuckle on the cheek. <S id="banana" /> peels one index finger with the other hand. <S id="bread" /> slices a flat hand with the other hand. <S id="pizza" /> has several versions; the clip shows a common one that draws a Z with a P-like handshape, or a bent V bringing a slice to the mouth.

<Signs ids="cake,chocolate" columns={2} />

<S id="cake" /> rises a claw hand off the palm, batter rising. <S id="chocolate" /> circles a C on the back of the other hand.

The full lists are in [Fruit](/dictionary/fruit), [Vegetables](/dictionary/vegetables), [Protein and Dairy](/dictionary/protein-dairy), and [Snacks and Drinks](/dictionary/snacks-drinks).

## Ordering and asking

To ask what someone wants: <Gloss>YOU WANT WHAT?</Gloss> with the eyebrows down. To offer: <Gloss>YOU WANT COFFEE?</Gloss> with the eyebrows up. To answer: <Gloss>COFFEE PLEASE</Gloss>, or <Gloss>WATER, THANK-YOU</Gloss>. To say you like something: <S id="ilike" /> and the food. To say it was good: <S id="delicious" /> and a satisfied face.

## Practice

Narrate your next meal. HUNGRY. I EAT ... and sign each thing on the plate. DRINK WATER. FULL. DELICIOUS. It takes a minute, nobody has to see it, and it is one of the fastest ways to make food signs automatic.
