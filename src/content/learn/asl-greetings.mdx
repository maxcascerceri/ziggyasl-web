---
title: ASL Greetings
---

Greetings are where every signed conversation starts, and they come with a piece of etiquette that English speakers do not have: you cannot say someone's name to get their attention. You have to be seen first. So the real first step of any ASL greeting is a wave in the person's line of sight, a light tap on the shoulder, or a flick of the lights in a room. Then you sign hello.

## Hello and goodbye

<Signs ids="hello,bye" columns={2} />

<S id="hello" /> is a salute that opens outward from the forehead, or just a friendly wave. Both are fine and both are common. <S id="bye" /> is a wave, often with the fingers folding down. These are two of the simplest signs in the language, and they are used constantly.

## Good morning, good night

<Signs ids="goodmorning,goodnight" columns={2} />

<S id="goodmorning" /> is GOOD followed by MORNING, where the morning hand rises like the sun over the other arm. <S id="goodnight" /> is GOOD followed by NIGHT, where the hand sets over the arm. There is also a good afternoon, where the hand sits at the top of its arc; see <S id="afternoon" />.

## Introducing yourself

<Signs ids="mynameis,whatisyourname,nicetomeetyou" columns={3} />

The full introduction is <S id="hello" />, <S id="mynameis" />, your fingerspelled name, then <S id="nicetomeetyou" />. If you want to ask their name back, <S id="whatisyourname" /> ends with WHAT and a lowered-eyebrow question face.

<S id="nicetomeetyou" /> is NICE, a flat hand sliding across the other palm, then MEET, two index fingers coming together. The two signs flow into one motion.

<Note title="Teacher's note">
After you spell your name, expect to be asked if you have a name sign. Most new learners do not, and the honest answer is a head shake and NO. Name signs are given, usually by Deaf friends, not chosen. See our <a href="/learn/deaf-culture-basics">Deaf culture basics</a> for how that works.
</Note>

## How are you, and the honest answers

<Signs ids="howareyou,imfine" columns={2} />

<S id="howareyou" /> is HOW, two bent hands rolling open, then YOU. The answer is usually <S id="imfine" /> with the face carrying the truth: a bright face means good, a tired face means tired. You can also answer with any feeling from our [feelings guide](/learn/asl-feelings).

## Meeting again, and saying goodbye for now

<Signs ids="nicetoseeyou,seeyoulater,seeyoutomorrow,talktoyoulater" columns={2} />

<S id="nicetoseeyou" /> is for people you already know. <S id="seeyoulater" /> and <S id="seeyoutomorrow" /> both start with the SEE sign, a V at the eyes, moving forward. <S id="talktoyoulater" /> is a casual sign-off between friends.

<Signs ids="welcome,havegoodday" columns={2} />

<S id="welcome" /> sweeps an open hand toward your body, inviting someone in. <S id="havegoodday" /> is a warm, slightly formal goodbye.

## A full greeting, start to finish

Put it together. Wave to get attention. <S id="hello" />. <S id="mynameis" /> and spell your name. <S id="nicetomeetyou" />. <S id="howareyou" />. Watch their answer. When it is time to go, <S id="seeyoulater" /> and a wave.

That is a complete exchange, and you can do all of it in your first week. Sign it in the mirror until it flows without stopping between signs.
