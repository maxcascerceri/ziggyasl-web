---
title: ASL Classes vs Apps
---

Classes and apps are good at different things. Knowing which is which saves you money and frustration, and helps you decide what to start with.

| | App | Class or tutor |
|---|---|---|
| Daily practice | Easy, anytime | Usually weekly |
| Vocabulary | Fast | Slower |
| Feedback on your signing | Limited | Yes |
| Real conversation | Rarely | Yes |
| Deaf culture | Some | Lots, especially with a Deaf teacher |
| Schedule | Yours | Fixed |
| Cost | Usually lower | Usually higher |

## When an app is the right start

You're a beginner, your schedule is unpredictable, or you want to learn the alphabet and your first 100 signs before walking into a class. Apps are also the easiest way to keep up daily review between classes. If you're choosing one, see [the best apps to learn sign language](/best-asl-apps).

## When you need a class

You want to hold real conversations, you need a certificate or course credit, or you've plateaued and need someone to correct your signing. Look for a class taught by a Deaf instructor if you can, through a community college, a Deaf community center, or an online program.

## The combination that works

A few minutes a day in an app like Ziggy, one class or meetup a week, and our [free dictionary](/dictionary) for anything you forget. Before your first class, it helps to know the [alphabet](/learn/asl-alphabet) and how to introduce yourself.
