---
title: ASL Question Words
---

ASL asks questions with the face first and the hands second. A WH-question (what, where, when, who, why, how, which) gets lowered eyebrows and a slight forward tilt of the head. A yes-or-no question gets raised eyebrows. The hands make the same signs either way; the eyebrows tell the listener what kind of answer you want. Beginners who skip the eyebrows get confused looks, because without them a question reads as a statement.

The second thing to know: the question word usually comes at the end of the sentence. English says "Where is the bathroom?" ASL says BATHROOM WHERE, with the eyebrows down on WHERE and held there until the other person answers.

## The six WH-signs

<Signs ids="what,where,when,who,why,how" columns={3} />

<S id="what" /> is open hands with a small shake, or an index finger sliding across the other palm. <S id="where" /> is an index finger wagging side to side. <S id="when" /> circles an index finger around the other index finger and lands on it. <S id="who" /> is a bent index finger at the chin. <S id="why" /> touches the forehead and pulls down into a Y. <S id="how" /> rolls two bent hands open.

Learn all six as a set, each with lowered eyebrows. Say the English word under your breath while you sign it to lock in the pairing.

## Which and how many

<Signs ids="which,howmany" columns={2} />

<S id="which" /> alternates two thumbs up and down, like weighing options. <S id="howmany" /> starts as closed fists and springs the fingers open, with a questioning face.

<Note title="Teacher's note">
Hold the question face until you get an answer. In English we drop our voice at the end of a question and then wait in silence. In ASL you hold your eyebrows and lean slightly forward. That holding is how the other person knows the floor is theirs.
</Note>

## Questions you will actually ask

<Signs ids="whatisyourname,whereareyoufrom,howoldareyou" columns={3} />

<S id="whatisyourname" /> is YOU NAME WHAT. <S id="whereareyoufrom" /> is YOU FROM WHERE. <S id="howoldareyou" /> is YOU OLD HOW-MANY, signed from the chin. Notice that every one of them puts the question word last.

<Signs ids="whatareyoudoing,whathappened,whatsthat,whereareyou" columns={2} />

<S id="whatareyoudoing" /> is DO-DO with a questioning face. <S id="whathappened" /> is HAPPEN WHAT. <S id="whatsthat" /> points and asks. <S id="whereareyou" /> is YOU WHERE, and it is how you text a friend at a crowded event.

## Yes-or-no questions

Any statement becomes a yes-or-no question with raised eyebrows. <S id="you" /> <S id="deaf" /> with raised eyebrows means "Are you Deaf?" <S id="you" /> <S id="hungry" /> with raised eyebrows means "Are you hungry?" There is no DO or ARE sign to add. The face is the grammar.

## Answering

Short answers are fine and normal. <S id="yes" />, <S id="no" />, <S id="dontknow" />, <S id="maybe" />, <S id="notyet" />. A nod or head shake while you repeat the key sign is also a complete answer: HUNGRY with a nod means "yes, I'm hungry."

## Practice

Ask yourself five questions a day in the mirror, with the eyebrows. YOU NAME WHAT. YOU FROM WHERE. YOU OLD HOW-MANY. TODAY WHAT-DAY. BATHROOM WHERE. Then answer each one. Within a week the eyebrows will come on their own.
